$24 an Hour Is How Much a Year?

$49,920 a year, before tax

$24 an hour is $49,920 a year before tax, at 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. That is the gross figure, before any tax comes out.

Federal tax, Social Security and Medicare come out in every state; your state decides the rest. On the same $49,920, Florida leaves you $42,291 and Oregon $38,725 — a gap of $3,566 a year, or $297.14 a month, on identical pay.

Where $24 an hour sits in the US labor market

Across all US occupations the median wage is $24.51 an hour and the mean is $33.54 — Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025 data covering 155,495,730 jobs, released May 15, 2026.

At $24 an hour you are about 2% below the national median. More than half of US jobs pay more than this, so the figures below describe pay in the lower half of the national distribution.

Annualized, $49,920 compares with a national mean of $69,770 a year. The median and the mean differ because a minority of very high-paying occupations pulls the average up; the median is the better description of a typical job.

How close $24 an hour is to the next federal bracket

The gross conversion stops at $49,920. The number that decides what a raise is actually worth is a different one: where $49,920 falls in the federal brackets.

After the $16,100 standard deduction, $49,920 leaves $33,820 of taxable income for a single filer. That sits in the 12% federal bracket, which runs to $50,400 of taxable income. Only part of it is taxed at that rate: the first $12,400 is taxed at the 10% rate and only the last $21,420 at 12%. A bracket applies to the dollars above its threshold, never to the whole salary. The “effective rate” column in the table below measures something different again: the share of gross pay lost to every deduction together — federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare at 7.65% combined, and any state income tax. There is $16,580 of room left before the next dollar would be taxed at 22% — about 691 more hours at $24 an hour, or a raise of about $7.97 an hour, held for a full year.

Questions

$24 an hour is how much a year?
$24 an hour is $49,920 a year before tax, at 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. After tax it depends on where you work: Florida leaves you $42,291 and Oregon $38,725, a difference of $3,566 on identical pay.
$24 an hour is how much a year after taxes?
Between $38,725 and $42,291 depending on the state, for a single filer taking the standard deduction in 2026. That is an effective rate of 15.3 to 22.4 percent. The table above shows every state we publish, and each figure is the same arithmetic our paycheck calculator runs, not a rounded estimate.
$24 an hour is how much a month?
$4,160.00 a month before tax. After tax, between $3,227.09 and $3,524.23 a month depending on the state. Note that a month is not four weeks: twelve monthly payments of $4,160.00 and twenty-six biweekly payments of $1,920.00 come to the same $49,920.
$24 an hour is how much a week?
$960.00 a week before tax at 40 hours, and $1,920.00 for a biweekly pay period. Overtime, unpaid leave and short weeks all move this figure, which is why the annual total on this page assumes a full 2,080 hours.
Is 2,080 hours the right number to multiply by?
It is the convention — 40 hours a week for 52 weeks — and it assumes no unpaid time off. It is not the law. At 35 hours a week $24 an hour comes to $43,680 a year, and at 30 hours $37,440. The table of weekly hours above gives the honest range instead of one number.
Why is the after-tax figure lower than the gross yearly figure?
Because tax comes out of your pay before it reaches you. This page uses 2,080 paid hours a year and takes out federal tax, Social Security and Medicare, so the figure is closer to a paycheck than the gross conversion. The state table also subtracts each state's own withholding where it has one.
Does the state really change the answer that much?
On $49,920 a year, the gap between Florida and Oregon is $3,566 a year, or $297.14 a month, which is 7.1% of gross pay. Federal tax and Social Security and Medicare come out in every state. Hours matter as much as the rate: overtime, unpaid leave and short weeks all change the total.
