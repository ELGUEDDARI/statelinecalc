$26 an Hour Is How Much a Year?

$54,080 a year, before tax

$26 an hour is $54,080 a year before tax, at 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. That is the gross figure, before any tax comes out.

Federal tax, Social Security and Medicare come out in every state; your state decides the rest. On the same $54,080, Florida leaves you $45,633 and Oregon $41,718 — a gap of $3,915 a year, or $326.26 a month, on identical pay.

Where $26 an hour sits in the US labor market

Across all US occupations the median wage is $24.51 an hour and the mean is $33.54 — Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025 data covering 155,495,730 jobs, released May 15, 2026.

At $26 an hour you are about 6% above the national median. More than half of US jobs pay less than this. The national mean of $33.54 is still higher, but a mean is pulled up by the best-paid occupations rather than by typical pay.

Annualized, $54,080 compares with a national mean of $69,770 a year. The median and the mean differ because a minority of very high-paying occupations pulls the average up; the median is the better description of a typical job.

How close $26 an hour is to the next federal bracket

The gross conversion stops at $54,080. The number that decides what a raise is actually worth is a different one: where $54,080 falls in the federal brackets.

After the $16,100 standard deduction, $54,080 leaves $37,980 of taxable income for a single filer. That sits in the 12% federal bracket, which runs to $50,400 of taxable income. Only part of it is taxed at that rate: the first $12,400 is taxed at the 10% rate and only the last $25,580 at 12%. A bracket applies to the dollars above its threshold, never to the whole salary. The “effective rate” column in the table below measures something different again: the share of gross pay lost to every deduction together — federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare at 7.65% combined, and any state income tax. There is $12,420 of room left before the next dollar would be taxed at 22% — about 478 more hours at $26 an hour, or a raise of about $5.97 an hour, held for a full year.

$26 an hour after tax in four states

Here are four of the 50 states in the table below, for a single filer at $26 an hour with 2026 rates. The state that takes the most at this pay is Oregon: $3,915 a year in state and local taxes and state payroll programs, which leaves $41,718 in take-home pay, or $20.06 an hour. The state closest to the middle of the 50 is Montana, where the same pay leaves $43,848; the median across all 50 is $43,852. Of the states that take something, the lightest is Alaska at $270 a year, leaving $45,363. 8 of the 50 withhold nothing for the state at this pay; Texas stands for them, with $45,633 a year, or $3,802.77 a month.

Side by side, Oregon and Texas are $3,915 a year apart on identical pay, which is 7.2% of gross. Montana sits $1,785 below Texas.

The same $26 an hour for a single filer, a couple or a head of household

A single filer, the standard deduction of $16,100 leaves $37,980 taxable, and your top dollar of taxable income is taxed in a 12% bracket, with $12,420 of taxable income to go before the next bracket. Federal tax comes to $4,310 and take-home pay in a state with no income tax is $45,633. Married filing jointly, the standard deduction of $32,200 leaves $21,880 taxable, and your top dollar of taxable income is taxed in a 10% bracket, with $2,920 of taxable income to go before the next bracket. Federal tax comes to $2,188 and take-home pay in a state with no income tax is $47,755. As head of household, the standard deduction of $24,150 leaves $29,930 taxable, and your top dollar of taxable income is taxed in a 12% bracket, with $37,520 of taxable income to go before the next bracket. Federal tax comes to $3,238 and take-home pay in a state with no income tax is $46,705.

The gap between a single filer and a married couple filing jointly is $2,122 of federal tax a year. It comes entirely from the deduction and the bracket widths, because Social Security and Medicare ($4,137) do not depend on filing status. The married figure assumes one earner at this rate and no other income, and head of household applies only if you qualify for it.

What a $26 an hour job pays at 35, 45 or 50 hours a week

The yearly figure above assumes 40 hours. Counting hours over 40 at time and a half, and a state with no income tax: 35 hours a week is $47,320 a year gross and $40,202 net, or $773.11 a week; 40 hours a week is $54,080 a year gross and $45,633 net, or $877.56 a week; 45 hours a week is $64,220 a year gross and $53,781 net, or $1,034.25 a week; 50 hours a week is $74,360 a year gross and $61,142 net, or $1,175.81 a week. Going from 40 to 50 hours moves your top dollar from the 12% bracket into the 22% bracket, so part of the overtime pay is taxed at a higher federal rate than regular pay.

What a $1 an hour raise from $26 is worth after tax

A raise to $27 an hour adds $2,080 of gross pay a year. In a state with no income tax that raise adds $1,671 to take-home pay, which is 80 cents of each extra dollar. In Oregon it adds $1,497, or 72 cents of each extra dollar. State taxes take a bigger share of the raise there, so it is worth $174 a year less than in a state with no income tax.

Putting 6% of $26 an hour into a 401(k)

A 6% traditional 401(k) contribution is $3,245 a year. It lowers federal taxable pay by the same amount, so federal tax falls from $4,310 to $3,920, a saving of $389. Social Security and Medicare are still charged on the full $54,080. Take-home pay drops from $45,633 to $42,778, which is $2,855 less in your paychecks for $3,245 saved, so each dollar you put away costs 88 cents of take-home pay. A Roth contribution does not lower this year's tax.

Questions

$26 an hour is how much a year?
$26 an hour is $54,080 a year before tax, at 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. After tax it depends on where you work: Florida leaves you $45,633 and Oregon $41,718, a difference of $3,915 on identical pay.
$26 an hour is how much a year after taxes?
Between $41,718 and $45,633 depending on the state, for a single filer taking the standard deduction in 2026. That is an effective rate of 15.6 to 22.9 percent. The table above shows every state we publish, and each figure is the same arithmetic our paycheck calculator runs, not a rounded estimate.
$26 an hour is how much a month?
$4,506.67 a month before tax. After tax, between $3,476.52 and $3,802.77 a month depending on the state. Note that a month is not four weeks: twelve monthly payments of $4,506.67 and twenty-six biweekly payments of $2,080.00 come to the same $54,080.
$26 an hour is how much a week?
$1,040.00 a week before tax at 40 hours, and $2,080.00 for a biweekly pay period. Overtime, unpaid leave and short weeks all move this figure, which is why the annual total on this page assumes a full 2,080 hours.
Is 2,080 hours the right number to multiply by?
It is the convention — 40 hours a week for 52 weeks — and it assumes no unpaid time off. It is not the law. At 35 hours a week $26 an hour comes to $47,320 a year, and at 30 hours $40,560. The table of weekly hours above gives the honest range instead of one number.
Why is the after-tax figure lower than the gross yearly figure?
Because tax comes out of your pay before it reaches you. This page uses 2,080 paid hours a year and takes out federal tax, Social Security and Medicare, so the figure is closer to a paycheck than the gross conversion. The state table also subtracts each state's own withholding where it has one.
Does the state really change the answer that much?
On $54,080 a year, the gap between Florida and Oregon is $3,915 a year, or $326.26 a month, which is 7.2% of gross pay. Federal tax and Social Security and Medicare come out in every state. Hours matter as much as the rate: overtime, unpaid leave and short weeks all change the total.
