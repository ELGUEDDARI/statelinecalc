Why the number will not match your pay stub exactly

Two different things get called “what I pay in tax.” Your withholding is a running estimate your employer makes each pay period from your W-4. Your liability is the final figure settled when you file. They are rarely identical, which is why refunds and balances due exist at all.

Our calculators estimate liability on a simplified wage situation. The most common reasons your real pay stub shows something different:

  • Pre-tax deductions. A 401(k), 403(b), HSA or FSA contribution and most employer health premiums come out before federal income tax is figured, which lowers the tax you actually pay.
  • Tax credits. The Child Tax Credit, the Earned Income Tax Credit and education credits reduce tax owed dollar for dollar. We do not model them.
  • Itemized deductions. We apply the standard deduction. If you itemize, your taxable income differs.
  • Your W-4 settings. Extra withholding, multiple-jobs adjustments and dependant entries all change what your employer takes out.
  • Multiple jobs or a working spouse. Each employer withholds as though it were your only income, which routinely under-withholds across a year.
  • Non-wage income. Self-employment, dividends, interest, capital gains and rental income are outside these tools entirely.
  • Local taxes. Some cities and counties levy their own income taxes, which are set locally and are not included here.
  • Pay-period rounding and mid-year changes. A raise, a bonus, unpaid leave or hitting the Social Security wage base mid-year all shift individual paychecks.

A useful rule of thumb: treat our figure as a solid estimate of the shape of your take-home pay, and treat a difference of a few percent from your actual stub as normal rather than as an error.

Rates can change after we publish

Every rate we use is read from an official source and published with the date it was checked — see the methodology page for the full list. Tax law still changes, agencies still revise figures, and a page can go stale between reviews. If our figure and the agency’s figure disagree, the agency is right. Please report it so we can fix it.

Where this matters most

Be especially careful about relying on an estimate if you are:

  • deciding whether to accept a job offer in another state;
  • setting your own withholding or estimated quarterly payments;
  • working out whether you can afford a mortgage or a lease;
  • self-employed, or paid partly in equity or commission;
  • living in one state and working in another, or working remotely across state lines.

Every one of those has details our calculators do not model.
