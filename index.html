Paycheck Salary to Hourly Methodology

Know what actually lands in your account

Your gross salary is not your pay. Between the federal brackets, FICA and whatever your state adds on top, the number that reaches your bank account is a different number entirely. These calculators show you that one — free, instantly, with no sign-up and no personal data required.

Start with your state

Every state taxes wages differently, and nine of them do not tax wages at all. Pick yours and see the arithmetic laid out line by line.

  • Texas paycheck calculator — nothing at state level, and the ban is constitutional. On $60,000 you keep about $50,390.
  • Florida paycheck calculator — nothing at state level either, but its constitution sets a ceiling rather than an outright ban. On $60,000 you keep about $50,390.
  • Nevada paycheck calculator — nothing at all comes out at state level, and no payroll programme either. On $60,000 you keep about $50,390.
  • Washington paycheck calculator — no income tax, but Paid Leave and WA Cares still come out of every check. On $60,000 you keep about $49,558.
  • Ohio paycheck calculator — nothing on the first $26,050 of taxable income, then 2.75% — the lowest state income tax on this site. On $60,000 you keep about $49,184, before any city tax.
  • Pennsylvania paycheck calculator — a flat 3.07% from the first dollar, and the one state here where a 401(k) does not cut your state tax. On $60,000 you keep about $48,506.
  • Georgia paycheck calculator — a flat 4.99% after a $15,000 standard deduction, and its own rules on tips and overtime. On $60,000 you keep about $48,145.
  • Michigan paycheck calculator — a flat 4.25% after a $5,900 exemption per person, and a number of cities add their own tax on top. On $60,000 you keep about $48,091.
  • Utah paycheck calculator — a flat 4.45% on every dollar, with no deduction at all — relief comes as a $485 credit that fades out and is gone by $46,656. On $60,000 you keep about $47,720.
  • Illinois paycheck calculator — a flat 4.95% after a $2,925 personal exemption that vanishes above $250,000. On $60,000 you keep about $47,565.

Ten states are live now, single filer, 2026 rates. More are being added — see the full state index. There is also a salary to hourly calculator for each of them, which gives the after-tax hourly rate rather than only the gross one.

Or start from the hourly rate

Divide a salary by 2,080 hours and you get the gross hourly rate: on $60,000 that is $28.85 everywhere, because arithmetic does not vary by state. What varies is what survives federal tax, FICA and whatever the state takes on top.

  • Texas salary to hourly — Nothing comes out at state level, so the gap between gross and net is federal tax and FICA alone. You are left with $24.23 an hour.
  • Florida salary to hourly — No state income tax either, so an hour is worth the same here as in Texas. You are left with $24.23 an hour.
  • Nevada salary to hourly — No income tax and no state payroll programme, which is as close to gross as an hourly rate gets. You are left with $24.23 an hour.
  • Washington salary to hourly — No income tax, but Paid Leave and WA Cares take their cut of every hour. You are left with $23.83 an hour.
  • Pennsylvania salary to hourly — A flat 3.07% from the first dollar, with no standard deduction to soften it. You are left with $23.32 an hour.
  • Georgia salary to hourly — A flat 4.99%, though a $15,000 standard deduction lands first. You are left with $23.15 an hour.
  • Michigan salary to hourly — A flat 4.25% after a $5,900 exemption, before any city tax. You are left with $23.12 an hour.
  • Illinois salary to hourly — A flat 4.95% and only a $2,925 exemption, which is why it is last on this list. You are left with $22.87 an hour.

Same eight states, single filer, 2026 rates, 40 hours a week. The full list is on the salary to hourly calculator page, and each one shows the deduction-by-deduction arithmetic behind its figure.

Or come at it from the other direction: $25 an hour is how much a year? — $52,000 before tax, and between $41,533 and $43,962 after it, depending on which state you work in. The same question is answered for $27, $30 and $35 an hour, each set against the US median wage of $24.51 an hour.

How this site is different

Every rate carries its source and the date it was checked. Tax figures change every year, and a number without a date is a number you cannot trust. Ours come from the IRS, the Social Security Administration and the relevant state agencies, and each page says when it was last verified.

Nothing you type leaves your browser. The calculations run on your own device. We do not store your salary, we do not ask for an email, and there is no account to create. A money calculator has no business collecting personal data.

The numbers are shown as a table too, not just a tool. Every calculator page publishes a full table of results across salary levels, so you can find yourself without typing anything — and so search engines and AI assistants can actually read the answer.

What we do not do

We do not give financial, tax or legal advice, and we do not pretend a calculator can replace an accountant. These tools use the standard deduction and published rates. They do not model itemized deductions, tax credits, multiple jobs, or your employer's benefit plan. For anything consequential, talk to a qualified professional.
