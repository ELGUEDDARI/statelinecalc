Florida Paycheck Calculator 2026

Florida takes nothing out of your paycheck. There is no state income tax, no state disability insurance and no paid leave premium, so every dollar withheld from a Florida check is federal: income tax, Social Security and Medicare. On $75,000, a single filer keeps about $61,593 a year.

How this calculator works

A Florida paystub has no state line on it. There is no state income tax to withhold, no city or county wage tax underneath it, and no state payroll program deducted from your pay the way Washington deducts two. What is left is federal, and only federal.

The calculator applies three deductions, in this order:

  • Federal income tax. Your gross pay minus the standard deduction for your filing status, run through the 2026 federal brackets. For 2026 the standard deduction is $16,100 for a single filer, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for head of household.
  • Social Security at 6.2% of gross wages, up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Earnings above that are not subject to it.
  • Medicare at 1.45% of all wages, with no cap, plus the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on wages above $200,000.

That is the whole list. Rates come from the agencies that set them: the IRS for the federal brackets, the standard deduction and FICA, cross-checked against the Social Security Administration for the wage base. The Florida side was read from the state’s own sources — the Florida Constitution on the Florida Senate’s site, and the Florida Department of Revenue for the reemployment tax — on August 28, 2026. Our full sourcing is on the methodology page.

What the calculator deliberately does not do: it does not itemize deductions, apply tax credits, model multiple jobs, or account for health insurance premiums and other employer benefit deductions. Those are personal to your W-4 and your benefits election, and guessing at them would make the number look precise while being wrong.

Florida hourly paycheck: what your rate is worth after tax

Many of the people asking this question are paid by the hour, not by the year. The calculator above takes an hourly rate directly — switch How often you are paid to Per hour, tell it how many hours a week you actually work, and it will show your take-home pay per hour, per week or per year.

Florida makes this arithmetic unusually clean. Because the state withholds nothing, your net hourly rate is your gross rate minus federal tax and FICA, and nothing else. The same $25 an hour in Georgia nets $20.25 after the state takes its 4.99%, against $21.14 here.

It matters more in Florida than in most states. Hospitality, restaurants, theme parks, retail, construction and healthcare shifts are all paid by the hour, and all of them run on schedules that move week to week. Your real annual income depends on hours you do not fully control.

Why we ask your hours instead of assuming 2,080

Most hourly calculators multiply your rate by 2,080 and call it your salary. That number assumes exactly 40 hours a week, 52 weeks a year, with no unpaid time off and no slow weeks — which is precisely what hourly work often is not. At 35 hours a week the same rate produces a materially smaller annual figure, and sometimes a lower tax bracket with it. We ask rather than assume.

What $20 an hour comes to in Florida

At 40 hours a week, $20 an hour is $35,606 a year after tax, or about $2,967.13 a month. Your effective rate is 14.4%, and your real take-home rate is $17.12 an hour. That is the number worth carrying in your head when you are comparing job offers.

What $25 and $30 an hour come to

At $25 an hour you take home about $43,962 a year, a real rate of $21.14. At $30 it is $52,318, a real rate of $25.15. Notice that a $5 raise on paper is worth less than $5 in your pocket — that is progressive federal taxation doing exactly what it is designed to do, with no state layer on top.

Tips: the deduction that matters most in this state

Florida taxes no wage income, so the state does nothing to your tips at all. Federally, 2026 is different from what most people expect, and this calculator does not model it. Two deductions created by the One, Big, Beautiful Bill run from 2025 through 2028:

  • Qualified tips — up to $25,000 a year deductible, for people in occupations that customarily and regularly receive tips.
  • Qualified overtime — up to $12,500 a year ($25,000 filing jointly). It covers only the premium half of time-and-a-half required by the Fair Labor Standards Act, not the whole overtime paycheck.

Both phase out above $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($300,000 filing jointly), and both are available whether you itemize or take the standard deduction. They are claimed on your return, on Schedule 1-A — not through your paycheck. So the figure above still reflects what comes out during the year; if a meaningful part of your pay is tips, your final tax bill may be lower than it implies. Source: IRS, Working Families Tax Cuts, read August 28, 2026.

Overtime and shift differentials in your withholding

For withholding purposes, overtime, reported tips and shift or holiday differentials are ordinary wages: your employer withholds federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare on them exactly as on your base rate. A big overtime week can push that single paycheck into a higher withholding band, which is why it sometimes looks disappointing on the stub. Enter your realistic average hours, including overtime, for the most useful figure.

Key facts that affect your take-home pay in Florida

The constitution sets a ceiling, not a flat ban

This is a real distinction, and almost every page that lists the no-tax states blurs it. Article VII, Section 5(a) of the Florida Constitution says no tax on “the income of natural persons who are residents or citizens of the state shall be levied by the state, or under its authority, in excess of the aggregate of amounts which may be allowed to be credited upon or deducted from any similar tax levied by the United States”. That is a limit tied to federal law — not the outright “may not impose” that Texas wrote into its constitution in 2019.

What that means for your paycheck, which is the part you care about

Nothing at state level comes out. The practical answer in Florida and Texas is identical today: no state income tax line, no state payroll program, nothing. The difference is in the legal machinery behind it, not in the number on your stub.

The corporate cap is a different subsection, and not about you

Subsection (b) of the same section caps tax on non-natural persons at 5% of net income. That is Florida’s corporate income tax. It has nothing to do with a wage earner’s paycheck, and it is the usual source of confusion when someone reads that “Florida has a 5% income tax”.

Reemployment tax is paid by your employer, not by you

Florida calls its unemployment tax something else. The Department of Revenue states that “reemployment tax is paid by employers” and that “only the first $7,000 of wages paid to each employee by their employer in a calendar year is taxable”. Nothing is deducted from your pay.

Why it is called reemployment tax at all

Legislation in 2012 renamed Florida’s Unemployment Compensation Law the Reemployment Assistance Program Law. The tax followed the name. If you have moved to Florida and cannot find the unemployment line on your paperwork, that is why.

$7,000 is the lowest wage base in the country

That figure is the federal floor for a state unemployment wage base; no state uses a lower one. It is an employer cost, so it never touches your paycheck — but it is one reason employers cite Florida when they talk about payroll costs.

No state disability insurance and no paid family leave premium

Several states deduct a percentage for short-term disability or paid family leave. Florida has neither program, so there is no such line. Compare that with Washington, which has no income tax either but still withholds two state programs from every check.

Social Security stops, Medicare does not

Social Security is charged at 6.2% only up to $184,500 of wages in 2026. Cross that line and your take-home pay jumps mid-year, because the deduction simply stops. Medicare has no such cap, and adds 0.9% above $200,000.

Your effective rate is lower than your bracket

Being “in the 22% bracket” does not mean 22% of your income goes in tax. Only the dollars above the bracket threshold are taxed at that rate. On $75,000 in Florida the total federal bite, FICA included, is 17.9% — and there is nothing above it.

Common mistakes people make

Reading “5% income tax” in the constitution and panicking

That 5% cap applies to non-natural persons — companies. Subsection (a), the one about people, is the one that governs your paycheck, and it produces no state withholding at all.

Treating the paycheck as the whole picture

Florida funds itself through sales tax, property tax and tourism rather than income tax. A larger paycheck can be offset by insurance and housing costs that are not on this page. Compare total cost of living, not net pay alone.

Assuming no income tax means an enormous difference

Against a neighboring state with an income tax it is real and measurable: a Florida worker on $75,000 keeps $2,994 a year more than the same worker in Georgia. Against Texas or Nevada the difference is zero.

Confusing the employer’s reemployment tax with a deduction

Employer reemployment rates are often quoted as though workers paid them. They do not. The Department of Revenue says the tax is paid by employers.

Using an annual figure when you are paid hourly

Multiplying an hourly rate by 2,080 assumes a full year of full weeks. If your hours vary, set the calculator to Per hour and enter your realistic average instead.

Expecting the calculator to match the paystub to the dollar

It will not, and no calculator can. Your employer withholds from your W-4, including any extra amount you asked for; we estimate your liability on a simplified wage situation. A gap of a few percent is normal. See the disclaimer.

Frequently asked questions

What is $20 an hour after taxes in Florida?

About $35,606 a year, or $2,967.13 a month, at 40 hours a week for a single filer in 2026. That is a real take-home rate of $17.12 an hour, an effective rate of 14.4 percent. Florida withholds nothing at state level, so all of it is federal.

Does Florida have a state income tax?

No. Article VII, Section 5(a) of the Florida Constitution bars the state from taxing the income of natural persons above what federal law allows to be credited or deducted against a similar tax. In practice nothing at state level is withheld from a Florida paycheck. Note that this is a ceiling rather than the outright prohibition Texas wrote into its constitution in 2019, even though the result on your paycheck today is identical.

What is take-home pay on a $75,000 salary in Florida?

About $61,593 a year, or roughly $5,133 a month, for a single filer taking the standard deduction in 2026. Total withholding is $13,407.50, an effective rate of 17.9 percent, and every dollar of it is federal.

Is Florida reemployment tax taken out of my paycheck?

No. The Florida Department of Revenue states that reemployment tax is paid by employers, and that only the first $7,000 of wages paid to each employee in a calendar year is taxable. Florida renamed its Unemployment Compensation Law the Reemployment Assistance Program Law in 2012, which is why the tax has an unusual name. None of it is withheld from your pay.

How much more do I keep in Florida than in Georgia?

On a $75,000 salary, $2,994 a year. A single filer keeps about $61,593 in Florida against $58,599 in Georgia, and the whole gap is Georgia’s flat 4.99% income tax. The federal tax and FICA are identical in both states.

Are tips taxed differently in Florida?

Not by Florida, which taxes no wage income at all. Federally, a deduction created for 2025 through 2028 allows up to $25,000 of qualified tips to be deducted on your return, phasing out above $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income. It is claimed on Schedule 1-A, not through payroll, so it does not change what comes out of your check during the year.

Why is my Florida paycheck smaller than the calculator says?

Almost always because of things that are personal to you rather than to Florida: health insurance premiums, a 401(k) contribution, extra withholding on your W-4, or a second job. The calculator models one job, the standard deduction and wages only.

Compare with neighboring states

Florida’s advantage only shows up in comparison. These are the states people actually weigh it against:

  • Georgia — directly to the north, and a flat 4.99% income tax. On $75,000 that is $2,994 a year of difference.
  • Texas — the same paycheck as Florida, with a constitutional ban rather than a ceiling.
  • Nevada — also nothing at state level, and the other big tourism and hospitality economy.
  • Alabama — a state income tax, and some municipal occupational taxes on top of it.
  • New York — the comparison behind a large share of moves into Florida, and the widest gap of any pair here.
