How this calculator works

  • Federal tax: the 2026 brackets after the standard deduction ($16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly, $24,150 head of household). The same everywhere.
  • FICA: Social Security at 6.2% up to $184,500 of wages, Medicare at 1.45% with no ceiling.
  • State tax: that state’s own rate, deduction and payroll programs, read from its tax agency. Local taxes are not included, except the Indiana and Maryland county tax.

Every rate sits next to its official source and the date we read it, on the methodology page. The calculator assumes one job and the standard deduction, and starts as a single filer: open “More options” to choose married filing jointly or head of household. It does not model credits, itemized deductions, a second job or pre-tax premiums beyond the 401(k) field.

Key facts

  • Nine states do not tax wages: Washington, Nevada, Texas, Tennessee, Florida, Alaska, Wyoming, New Hampshire, and South Dakota. Federal tax and FICA still come out there, and Washington adds Paid Family and Medical Leave and WA Cares.
  • The state is the difference. Two people on the same salary can end up thousands of dollars apart, because states differ in rate, deduction and what they withhold on top.
  • Paid by the hour? Change “Paid” to “Per hour”; we ask for your real weekly hours rather than assuming 2,080 a year. For a rate-first view, see what $25 an hour adds up to in a year or the salary to hourly calculator.
  • Two states side by side? The state comparison shows the difference in dollars.

Frequently asked questions

How much of my paycheck goes to taxes?

On a $75,000 salary, a single filer loses about 18 to 25 percent of gross pay to federal income tax, FICA and state tax, depending on the state (see the table above). Higher salaries lose a larger share. The federal portion and FICA are the same everywhere; the spread comes almost entirely from the state.

Which states have no income tax in 2026?

Washington, Nevada, Texas, Tennessee, Florida, Alaska, Wyoming, New Hampshire, and South Dakota — nine in total. None of them tax wages.

Does living in a no-income-tax state mean nothing comes out of my paycheck?

No. Federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare are withheld in every state, and some no-income-tax states run payroll programs of their own. Washington withholds Paid Family and Medical Leave and WA Cares from every check, and Alaska withholds unemployment insurance from employees.

Which state taxes my pay if I live in one state and work in another?

Usually both have a claim. The state where you work taxes the income earned there; your home state taxes your worldwide income and then credits the tax you already paid elsewhere. Some neighboring states have reciprocity agreements that let you be taxed only at home. This is the case where a simple calculator is least reliable and a professional is genuinely worth paying for.

Why does my real pay stub differ from the calculator?

Withholding is a running estimate your employer makes from your W-4; our figure is a simplified estimate of what you owe on wages alone. Pre-tax 401(k), HSA and health premiums, tax credits, itemized deductions, a second job and local taxes all move the real number. A difference of a few percent is normal.
