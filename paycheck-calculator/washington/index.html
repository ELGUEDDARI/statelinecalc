Washington Paycheck Calculator

Washington has no state income tax. On a $75,000 salary, a single filer takes home about $60,552 a year, or $5,046 a month, in 2026. The deductions are federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, WA Paid Family and Medical Leave (0.807%), and WA Cares (0.58%).

How this calculator works

Washington is one of nine states that does not tax wage income, so there is no state line on your paystub the way there is in Oregon or California. What remains is federal withholding, the two federal payroll taxes, and two state programs that are funded directly out of employee wages.

The calculator applies five deductions, in this order:

  • Federal income tax. Your gross pay minus the standard deduction for your filing status, run through the 2026 federal brackets. For 2026 the standard deduction is $16,100 for a single filer, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for head of household.
  • Social Security at 6.2% of gross wages, up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Earnings above that are not subject to it.
  • Medicare at 1.45% of all wages, with no cap, plus the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on wages above $200,000.
  • WA Paid Family and Medical Leave. The 2026 premium is 1.13% of gross wages, of which employees pay 71.43% — an effective employee rate of 0.807%, capped at the same $184,500 wage base.
  • WA Cares at 0.58% of gross wages, with no wage cap at all. This is the deduction that surprises high earners most.

Rates come directly from the agencies that set them: the IRS for federal brackets, the standard deduction and FICA; the Washington State Employment Security Department for the Paid Leave premium; and the WA Cares Fund for the long-term care premium. Every figure was verified on August 22, 2026. Our full sourcing is on the methodology page.

What the calculator deliberately does not do: it does not itemize deductions, apply tax credits, model multiple jobs, or account for health insurance premiums and other employer benefit deductions. Those are personal to your W-4 and your benefits election, and guessing at them would make the number look precise while being wrong.

Key facts that affect your take-home pay in Washington

No state income tax is worth more than most people assume. A single filer earning $100,000 keeps about $77,793 in Washington. The same salary in a state with a 5% flat income tax would lose roughly $4,200 more. Over a decade that difference buys a car.

The Social Security cap makes your effective rate fall, not rise, past $184,500. Once your wages pass the 2026 wage base, the 6.2% stops. It is the one point where earning more money genuinely lowers your marginal payroll burden — which is why the effective rate in the table above climbs steeply to $150,000 and then flattens.

WA Cares has no cap, and that is unusual. Every other line on a Washington paystub either caps out or is federal. WA Cares takes 0.58% of every dollar, forever. On $200,000 that is $1,160 a year. Workers who bought qualifying private long-term care insurance before the deadline could apply for an exemption; if you hold one, uncheck the box in the calculator.

Washington has no local income taxes either. Seattle, Spokane and Tacoma do not levy a city wage tax, so your ZIP code does not change the arithmetic. This is not true in Ohio, Pennsylvania, or New York City, where local tax can be several percent.

The trade-off is on the other side of the ledger. Washington funds itself with a sales tax that runs above 10% in much of the Puget Sound region, and with property taxes. A high earner comes out well ahead; the picture is less lopsided for someone spending most of what they earn.

Common mistakes people make

Comparing gross salaries across state lines. A $95,000 offer in Seattle and a $95,000 offer in Portland are not the same offer. Oregon's top marginal rate reaches 9.9%, and the Portland metro adds its own taxes on top. Compare take-home, not headline.

Forgetting that WA Cares and Paid Leave are not taxes you get back. They are insurance premiums. Nothing is refunded at filing time, and they do not appear as withholding on your federal return.

Assuming a bonus is taxed at a higher rate. Supplemental wages are usually withheld at a flat 22% federally, which is often more than your actual marginal rate. The excess comes back when you file. The bonus was never taxed at a penalty rate — it was over-withheld.

Entering take-home pay instead of gross. The single most common reason a calculator result looks wrong. The first field wants the number before anything is taken out.

Ignoring the 401(k) lever. Contributions reduce federal taxable income but not FICA, Paid Leave, or WA Cares. Putting 10% into a 401(k) on a $100,000 salary cuts roughly $2,200 from federal tax while leaving the state programs untouched. Try it in the calculator above.

Example calculation

Take a single filer in Tacoma earning $75,000 a year in 2026, with no retirement contribution. Here is every step.

Federal income tax. Taxable income is $75,000 minus the $16,100 standard deduction, or $58,900. That is taxed in three slices: 10% on the first $12,400 = $1,240; 12% on the next $38,000 (from $12,400 to $50,400) = $4,560; 22% on the remaining $8,500 (from $50,400 to $58,900) = $1,870. Federal tax is $7,670. Note that the 22% applies only to that last $8,500 — not to the whole salary.

Social Security. $75,000 is below the $184,500 wage base, so the full salary is taxed at 6.2% = $4,650.

Medicare. 1.45% of $75,000 = $1,087.50. The salary is below $200,000, so no Additional Medicare Tax applies.

WA Paid Family and Medical Leave. 0.807% of $75,000 = $605.37.

WA Cares. 0.58% of $75,000 = $435.

The result. Total withholding is $14,447.87, leaving take-home pay of $60,552.13 a year — $5,046.01 a month, or $2,329.00 every two weeks. The effective rate is 19.3%, even though the top marginal federal bracket touched is 22%.

Frequently asked questions

Does Washington have a state income tax?

No. Washington is one of nine states with no personal income tax on wages. Your paycheck still has federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, WA Paid Family and Medical Leave, and in most cases WA Cares withheld. A 7% capital gains tax exists on certain long-term gains above a threshold, but it does not touch wage income.

What is take-home pay on a $75,000 salary in Washington?

About $60,552 a year, or roughly $5,046 a month, for a single filer taking the standard deduction in 2026. Total withholding is about $14,448, an effective rate of 19.3%.

What is the WA Cares deduction on my paycheck?

WA Cares is Washington's long-term care program. Employees pay 0.58% of gross wages with no wage cap. On a $75,000 salary that is $435 a year, about $36.25 a month. It buys a lifetime benefit for long-term care costs, subject to vesting rules.

Why is my Washington paycheck smaller than the calculator says?

Usually health insurance premiums, retirement contributions beyond what you entered, or a W-4 that does not match your situation — for example a second job, or a spouse who also works. This calculator covers taxes and state payroll programs, not employer benefit deductions, which vary by employer.

Compare with neighboring states

Washington's advantage only shows up in comparison. These are the states people actually weigh it against:

  • Oregon — no sales tax, but income tax reaching 9.9%. The mirror image of Washington.
  • Idaho — a flat state income tax, lower cost of living.
  • California — the most common relocation comparison for tech workers, and the largest gap.
  • Nevada — also no income tax, and without Washington's Paid Leave and WA Cares premiums.
  • Texas — no income tax, higher property tax.
