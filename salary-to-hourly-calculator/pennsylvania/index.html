How the conversion works

Dividing by 2,080 is the convention: 40 hours a week, 52 weeks a year. It gives you the gross hourly rate, and it is the number every other page stops at. On $60,000 that is $28.85 an hour.

But nobody is paid gross. Before anything reaches you, four things come out in Pennsylvania:

  • Federal income tax, on your pay above the 2026 standard deduction of $16,100 for a single filer.
  • Social Security at 6.2%, up to a wage base of $184,500.
  • Medicare at 1.45% with no cap, plus 0.9% above $200,000.
  • What Pennsylvania withholds — set out on our Pennsylvania paycheck calculator, with each rate sourced and dated.

Do that and $60,000 stops being $28.85 an hour and becomes $23.32. The difference is $5.53 an hour — every hour, all year.

What Pennsylvania changes

Pennsylvania applies a flat 3.07% with no standard deduction and no personal exemption at all, so the state takes exactly 3.07% of every hour you work - the advertised rate and the real rate are the same number, which is rare. It also withholds 0.07% for unemployment, which most states take only from the employer. And one rule catches savers out: a 401(k) contribution cuts your federal tax but not your Pennsylvania tax, because the state counts the deferral as compensation the moment it is made.

How do you convert an annual salary to an hourly rate?

Divide by 2,080, which is 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. That gives the gross rate. To get the rate that actually reaches you, subtract federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare and whatever your state withholds first, then divide. The two numbers are not close: on $60,000 in Pennsylvania the difference is $5.53 an hour.

Is 2,080 hours the right number to divide by?

It is the convention, and it assumes 40 hours a week, 52 weeks a year, with no unpaid time off. If you work 35 hours a week the divisor is 1,820, and your real hourly rate is higher than 2,080 suggests while your annual income is lower. Enter your own hours in the calculator rather than accepting the assumption.

Why is the after-tax rate here lower than on other salary converters?

Because most converters stop at gross. Dividing a salary by 2,080 is arithmetic anyone can do; the useful question is what an hour is worth after federal tax, Social Security, Medicare and what Pennsylvania withholds. This page answers the second question, which is why its figure is lower and closer to your bank statement.

Does my hourly rate change if I am paid a salary?

Your pay does not change, but what an hour is worth to you does. A salaried job that quietly runs to 50 hours a week is paid at four fifths of the rate the same salary implies at 40. That is the comparison this page exists to make possible.
